What Does Your Tire Pressure Warning Light Really Mean & How to Fix It

By Alex Vance • Automotive Gear Specialist Updated for October 2026 • Roadside Safety Guide

That yellow horseshoe-shaped warning symbol with an exclamation point () appearing on your dashboard is your vehicle's Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) alert. Here is exactly what it signifies and what steps to take immediately.

3 Primary Reasons Your TPMS Warning Light Turned On

  1. Temperature Drop (Cold Weather Effect): When ambient temperatures fall overnight, tire air contracts. A 10°F drop typically reduces pressure by 1 PSI, triggering the sensor.
  2. Slow Puncture or Valve Leak: A nail or faulty valve stem causes a gradual loss of pressure over days or weeks.
  3. Under-Inflation Over Time: Tires naturally lose about 1 PSI per month even without punctures.

How to Do a Proper Tire Pressure Check at Home in 4 Easy Steps

By Marcus Thorne • Fleet Safety Director Updated for October 2026 • Maintenance Guide

Checking your tire pressure monthly extends tread life by up to 20%, improves fuel economy by 3%, and prevents hazardous highway blowouts. Here is how to conduct a accurate home check.

Step 1: Find Your Recommended Cold PSI

Do not read the maximum PSI listed on the tire sidewalk itself! Instead, open your driver-side door jamb and locate the manufacturer sticker (placard) for exact cold PSI specs (typically between 30 and 36 PSI).

Step 2: Always Check "Cold" Tires

Check tires first thing in the morning or at least 3 hours after driving. Driving heats the air inside tires, expanding pressure readings by 3 to 5 PSI and giving false elevated results.

Step 3: Measure & Adjust Pressure Effortlessly

Instead of juggling awkward pencil gauges and separate air pumps, screw the Airmoto air hose directly onto the valve stem. The digital screen displays live pressure instantly. If low, hit start to inflate to specification.
